Abstract
    CE




An analytical solution to model dynamic adsorption of multicomponent mixtures in packed beds is

presented. The analytical solution describes transient concentration profiles together with gas and
AC




solid phase temperature profiles during adsorption and desorption processes. The breakthrough

curves predicted by the analytical solution are in satisfactory agreement with numerical simulations

and experimental data reported in the literature.



Keywords Fixed bed, Modeling, Binary adsorption, Multicomponent adsorption, Breakthrough

1. Introduction

Adsorption in a fixed bed column includes multicomponent mass and heat transfer processes

combined with adsorption and desorption processes. The dynamic balance equations describe

transient concentration and temperature profiles in the adsorption column. The modeling of such

processes often relies on the numerical integration of the partial differential equations describing the

transfer processes in fixed-bed adsorption, which in certain cases results in numerical instabilities




                                                                T
and long calculation times. A number of analytical solutions of those systems of partial differential




                                                              IP
equations for the axial dispersion model (ADM) have been reported in the literature, but they are




                                                            CR
often limited to isothermal conditions and/or single-component systems (Ruthven, 1984; Tien, 1994).

Cooney and Strusi (1972) proposed an analytical solution for isothermal binary mixture adsorption

                                          US
in a fixed bed column defined by a plug flow model with axial dispersion. Miura et al. (1979)
                                        AN
developed a method for calculating the binary mixture breakthrough curves in a fixed-bed column

using an analytical solution of partial differential equations describing the transfer processes. Liaw et
                                 M


al. (1979) obtained an analytical solution of the partial differential equations for isothermal

adsorption based on the plug flow model taking into account the transfer processes in the adsorbent
                      ED




particle. Rasmuson and Neretnieks (1980, 1985) proposed an analytical solution of an axial
             PT




dispersion model in a fixed bed column with linear sorption in the particles. Seidel-Morgenstern

(1991) considered a fixed-bed adsorber with a linear adsorption isotherm and used Laplace inversion
    CE




with a Fourier series expansion to obtain an analytical solution and to determine the effect of

boundary conditions on the axial dispersion model. Liao and Shiau (2000) applied the separation of
AC




variables method to an axial dispersion model of a fixed-bed adsorber with a linear adsorption

isotherm and derived an analytical solution.

       A typical model of adsorption in the fixed-bed column includes (i) description of transfer

processes within an adsorbent pellet; (ii) balance equation for the adsorbate in the fluid phase; and

(iii) adsorption equilibrium equation coupling concentration of a component in fluid and solid phase.

Weber and Chakravorti (1974) provided a comparison of the analytical solution derived by Thomas
                            ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT


(1944) and Hiester and Vermuellen (1952), the homogeneous solid phase diffusion model of Rosen

(1952), the linear driving force method of Glueckauf (1955) and the pore phase diffusion model

(Edeskuty and Amundson, 1952; Kasten et al., 1952). They reviewed the various possible solutions

for the general case of a fixed bed with a homogeneous solid or pore diffusion model for the pellets.

For the case of constant pattern behavior with uniform velocity for the established temperature and

concentration fronts in the fixed-bed column, Ruthven et al. (1975) obtained the analytical solution




                                                                T
of the non-isothermal plug flow model with Langmuir adsorption isotherm.




                                                              IP
       The purpose of this study is to obtain an analytical solution for the case of binary and




                                                            CR
multicomponent adsorption in a fixed bed column taking into account the coupled heat and mass

transfer processes based on the axial dispersion model.

2. Axial dispersion model of adsorption
                                 M


The axial dispersion model (ADM) is a well-known model, based on an idealization of the flow

patterns in a fixed-bed column. The axial dispersion model of transfer processes developed in the
                      ED




current work is based on the following assumptions: (i) ideal gas flow in the column; (ii) Langmuir
             PT




type adsorption isotherm; (iii) a Linear Driving Force (LDF) model is used to describe mass transfer

in the solid phase; (iv) there is thermal equilibrium at the solid-gas interface; (v) mass and heat
    CE




transfer in the radial direction are negligible; (vi) there is a symmetrical concentration front profile

relative to the stoichiometric time. A theoretical framework for the concentration front during
AC




adsorption in a fixed bed column and the effect of the equilibrium isotherm has been given by De

Vault (1943) and Tondeur (1987).

developed axial dispersion model is based on dynamic balance equations taking into account the

adsorbed components concentration, temperature and component concentration in the gas phase. The

source terms in Table 1 take into account the interface transfer processes in fixed bed column.

4. Simulation Results and Discussion

Experimental breakthrough curves reflect the dynamics of the transfer process in fixed bed columns.

The shape of the breakthrough curve is a result of multicomponent mass transfer processes coupled

with adsorption and desorption processes in the column. The literature provides a large amount of
                           ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT


experimental data for fixed bed columns equipped with a different adsorbent (particles) under

different operating conditions. We used experimental data reported by Möller et al. (2017) with the

most comprehensive input data for axial dispersion model (ADM) parameters for a gas mixture

consisting of CO2/CH4/He and using the Shirasagi MSC CT Carbon Molecular Sieve adsorbent. The

chosen system corresponds to the case of Langmuir type binary adsorption from the gas phase by a

solid adsorbent. The necessary input data for calculating breakthrough curves are listed in Table 2.

        Available analytical solutions in the literature are often limited by the isothermal condition
              PT




and the number of components in the adsorption process. The new analytical solution is based on an

integration of the conservation equations using the new analytical solution for the driving force of
    CE




adsorption. The obtained analytical solution of the axial dispersion model (ADM) is suitable for

binary and multicomponent non-isothermal adsorption and desorption processes in fixed bed
AC




columns. The main advantages of the derived analytical solution of the axial dispersion model are

       Flexibility in the description of multicomponent adsorption and desorption processes;

       Flexibility in defining the adsorption equilibrium;

       Flexibility in the description of the coupled transfer processes without iterations;

       Flexibility in modeling adsorption process in fixed bed column with different flow patterns;

       Description of the transient concentration and temperature profiles in a fixed bed column.
                            ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT


Flexibility in adsorption equilibrium is due to the fact that any equilibrium model (explicit or

implicit models such as IAST) can be expressed through the equilibrium adsorbed concentration as a

function of concentration, temperature and time. The proposed analytical solution is applicable for

multicomponent adsorption and desorption processes. Further modification of this analytical solution

is possible in future taking into account additional effects (such as pressure and temperature

variations applied in PSA and TSA) on multicomponent adsorption and desorption processes in




                                                                T
fixed bed columns.

                                        AN

dispersion model (ADM) balance equations and analytical solution describe the transient
                                 M
