# Sensitivity Analysis by ANOVA of Clusters in Scatterplots

**Question.** How do inlet CO₂ concentration and volumetric flow rate affect breakthrough time and dynamic capacity, and which empirical model parameters carry that dependence?

**Method.** Model-independent one-variate sensitivity analysis after Kleijnen & Helton (1999a) and Saltelli et al. (2000, ch. 2). The output `y` is plotted against each input `xᵢ`; the `xᵢ` axis is partitioned into clusters; the null "all clusters share a common mean of `y`" is tested by one-way F (the **CMNs** test). Rejection means the scatterplot carries a non-random pattern, hence `xᵢ` is influential. No linearity between `y` and `xᵢ` is assumed anywhere — that is the point of using it in place of, or alongside, a regression. Kruskal–Wallis (the **CLs** test) is reported next to every F as the rank-based backup. Standardised regression coefficients (SRC) are reported for contrast; **where the cluster F is significant but the SRC is not, the dependence is non-linear and the regression has missed it.**

**Generated by:** `src/solver/sensitivity_anova.py` (seed 42), on artefacts from `src/solver/new_runs_pipeline.py`. Figures: `src/img/generated/sensitivity/`. All numeric tables: `src/docs/sensitivity_anova_tables.xlsx` (16 sheets).

> **This revision supersedes the 14-run version of 2026-08-05.** The basis grew from 9 to 16 usable `newest runs`, which introduces replication for the first time. Replication changes one headline conclusion — see §2.2 — and it does so in the direction of *less* confidence, not more.

## 2. Headline answer

### 2.1 What strengthened

**Flow rate sets *when* breakthrough happens, and the evidence is now much stronger.** On the 16-run grid, cluster ANOVA gives t_b: F(2,13) = 46.2, p = 1.2 × 10⁻⁶, η² = 0.877 — against F = 30.9, p = 7 × 10⁻⁴ at n = 9. Concentration remains n.s. for t_b marginally (p = 0.884). The two-way factorial pushes flow to F(2,7) = 145.3, p = 2.0 × 10⁻⁶.

**Two effects that were invisible at n = 9 are now resolved:**

- **L_MTZ responds to both factors** — flow F = 6.27, p = 0.014; concentration F = 4.89, p = 0.028. Both were n.s. before. (Read with §6.2 in mind: L_MTZ's dynamic range is still tiny.)
- **A flow × concentration interaction exists for t₅₀** — F(4,7) = 4.36, p = 0.044. This term was wholly confounded with error in the unreplicated design and could not previously be tested at all.

### 2.2 What did **not** survive replication

**The previous claim that "concentration sets capacity" does not hold up.** At n = 9 the concentration effect on q_dyn looked real: F = 5.29, p = 0.047, η² = 0.638. With 16 runs it drops to F = 3.75, **p = 0.052** in the cluster test and **p = 0.220** in the factorial. The reason is visible directly in the replicates:

| Q \ C₀ | 5 % | 10 % | 15 % |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 0.946 / 1.091 | 1.336 / **2.273** | 1.627 |
| 0.10 | 0.955 / 0.864 | 1.133 / **0.373** | 1.212 / 1.179 |
| 0.15 | 0.968 / **0.400** | 1.380 / **0.689** | 1.209 |

*q_dyn (mol/kg), replicates within a cell separated by "/"*

Nominally identical conditions give q_dyn differing by up to **3.04×**. Formally: **78.4 % of the total variance in q_dyn is pure measurement error**, median within-cell CV 36.7 %. There is no room left for a concentration effect to be detected — not because it is absent, but because the measurement cannot currently resolve it.

Contrast t_b, where pure error is only **4.4 %** of total variance (median within-cell CV 13.3 %). **The timing metrics are reproducible; the capacity metric is not.**

### 2.3 Summary verdict

| response | flow | concentration | interaction | measurement quality |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| t_b | **F = 145.3, p = 2.0 × 10⁻⁶** | F = 12.27, p = 0.0051 | n.s. (p = 0.133) | excellent (4.4 % pure error) |
| t₅₀ | **F = 145.8, p = 2.0 × 10⁻⁶** | F = 24.93, p = 0.00065 | **F = 4.36, p = 0.044** | excellent (4.0 %) |
| t_E | **F = 27.98, p = 0.00091** | F = 12.81, p = 0.0068 | n.s. (p = 0.296) | fair (16.0 %) |
| ψ | **F = 29.80, p = 0.00038** | n.s. (p = 0.343) | n.s. | fair (21.1 %) |
| L_MTZ | F = 9.22, p = 0.0148 | p = 0.082 . | n.s. | poor (37.0 %) + see §6.2 |
| **q_dyn** | n.s. (p = 0.114) | **n.s. (p = 0.220)** | n.s. | **unusable (78.4 %)** |

Physically the timing result is consistent: raising Q shortens residence time and drives the mass-transfer zone out of the bed sooner. The concentration effect on timing is real but second-order, and it only becomes visible once flow's variance is blocked out (§3.2). Whether concentration sets capacity is **currently untestable on this rig**.

## 4. Tier 2 — Monte Carlo sensitivity of the empirical parameters

Fitted parameters across the 21 runs define a range per parameter; N = 2000 draws by Latin hypercube, propagated through each closed form to all six responses at a **fixed reference operating point** — run `2026-07-08-conc10-flow0.10` (Q = 0.10 lpm, C₀ = 100 580 ppm, m = 8.00 g, L = 23.3 cm) — so only the parameters vary.

### 4.0 Two structural problems, both confirmed on the larger basis

**(a) `k₀` and `h` are not independently identifiable.** M11 and M10 enter as `k₀ · t^(−h) · (t − τ₀)`. Across 21 runs, ρ_Spearman(k₀, h) = **0.984** (M11) and **0.971** (M10) — see `S3_identifiability_ridge.png`. Independent-uniform sampling walks off that ridge: **11.3 % of M11 draws and 8.3 % of M10 draws produced no complete breakthrough curve at all**, versus **0 % under rank-correlated sampling**. Both schemes are reported: `independent` (the textbook baseline) and `rank-correlated` (Iman–Conover restricted pairing, Saltelli et al. 2000 §6.4).

The identifiable combination is `κ ≡ k₀ τ₀^(−h)`, the effective rate at the half-time. Any re-fit of M10/M11 should be parameterised on `(κ, τ₀, h)`.

**(b) M24 label switching, now shown to be optimiser-order-dependent.** `p·S(t;τ₁,k₁) + (1−p)·S(t;τ₂,k₂)` is invariant under swapping the components. 2 of 21 runs returned τ₁ > τ₂ and are canonicalised to τ₁ ≤ τ₂. Notably, `2026-07-08-conc5-flow0.15` was swapped in the previous fit and is *not* swapped in this one — the refit found the identical optimum (RSS unchanged) with the components returned in the opposite order, p → 1 − p. **The ordering is not reproducible run to run**, which is precisely why canonicalisation must be applied rather than trusted to the fitter.

Three readings that matter:

1. **`t₅₀` has R² = 1.000 exactly.** Not an artefact — for M10/M11 the argument `k₀t^(−h)(t−τ₀)` vanishes at `t = τ₀`, so `C/C₀ = ½` exactly there and `t₅₀ ≡ τ₀` analytically. A hard validation that the propagation code is correct.

2. **`t_E` is where the regression fails and the cluster ANOVA does not.** M11 independent: R² = 0.181, SEE = 16 810 s — the linear model explains almost nothing. Yet the cluster ANOVA finds `h` highly significant, F(4,1768) = 43.3, p = 1.0 × 10⁻³⁴. The dependence of exhaustion time on `h` is strongly non-monotone (visible in `S2_M11_independent.png`), so the SRC averages it to near zero while the cluster means separate cleanly. **This is the case for using this method at all.**

3. **Under correlated inputs the SRCs blow up** — standardised coefficients well outside [−1, 1] with opposite signs on `k₀` and `h`, the textbook multicollinearity artefact. The cluster F is untouched. Report the cluster ANOVA as primary and the SRC as secondary whenever inputs are correlated.

### 4.4 Parameter-by-parameter dissection

**M11 / M10 — `τ₀` (characteristic time, s).** Dominant for everything time-like: η² = 0.728 on t_b and 0.960 on t₅₀, where it *is* t₅₀ by identity. Range 223–3568 s, 1.2 decades. It absorbs essentially all of the flow dependence (§5). Interpretation: the stoichiometric time of the front — the time to load the bed to half capacity.

**M11 / M10 — `h` (fractal / anomalous-diffusion exponent).** Range 0.35–0.96. Controls the *shape* of the front, not its position: η² = 0.583 on L_MTZ and 0.496 on ψ, but 0.001 on t₅₀. h → 0 recovers the ordinary error-function (Fickian) front; h → 1 gives an effective rate decaying as 1/t, i.e. a strongly tailing sub-diffusive breakthrough. Fitted h of 0.62–0.96 in the newest runs says the measured fronts are markedly non-Fickian — consistent with intraparticle diffusion limitation in the PEI@SiO₂ pellets rather than film control.

**M11 / M10 — `k₀` (rate prefactor, s^(h−1)).** Not separately identifiable from `h` (§4.0a). Its apparent effects (η² 0.136–0.147 on L_MTZ and ψ) are the ridge's projection. **Do not report k₀ as an independent sensitivity.** Report `κ = k₀τ₀^(−h)`.

**M24 — `τ₁` (fast-component time constant, s).** Range 125–2688 s. Dominates t_b (η² = 0.553) and t₅₀ but is near-irrelevant to t_E. The fast component sets the *onset* of breakthrough.

**M24 — `τ₂` (slow-component time constant, s).** Range 686–6718 s. The mirror image: dominates t_E and q_dyn, weak on t_b (η² = 0.086). The slow component sets *saturation and capacity*. Together τ₁/τ₂ give the clearest physical decomposition among the 24 models: **a fast site population governing when CO₂ first appears at the outlet, and a slow population governing how much is ultimately held.**

**M24 — `p` (fast-component fraction).** Range 0.36–0.80. Uniquely **not significant for t_b (F = 1.06, p = 0.375)** while significant elsewhere. The mixing fraction cannot move the breakthrough onset because at small t both components are near zero regardless of weighting. The one clean null in Tier 2, and mechanistically sensible.

**M24 — `k₁`, `k₂` (component sharpness).** k₁ (1.31–6.75) drives ψ and L_MTZ strongly — sharpness of the fast front controls bed-use efficiency. k₂ (1.54–3.14) is the weakest parameter on every response: the slow tail's shape barely matters once its time constant is fixed. **k₂ remains a candidate for fixing at its median (2.28) in a future re-fit**, buying back a degree of freedom.

**The mechanism, stated end to end:**

- Flow rate compresses **all** the time constants near-proportionally — τ₀, τ₁ and τ₂ carry standardised coefficients of −0.84, −0.86 and −0.82 on Q, all at p ≤ 1.3 × 10⁻⁵. That is the whole of the flow → breakthrough-time effect, and it is why t_b, t₅₀ and t_E move together.

## 6. Limitations, flags, and what would change the conclusions

1. **Multiplicity.** Roughly 200 hypothesis tests are reported. Benjamini–Hochberg q-values are in the workbook. The strong results (p < 10⁻⁴) survive any correction; the borderline ones — the t₅₀ interaction at p = 0.044, C₀ → L_MTZ at p = 0.028, C₀ → q_dyn at p = 0.052 — do not survive a strict family-wise correction and should be treated as leads.
2. **L_MTZ has almost no dynamic range by construction.** `L_MTZ = [1 − (t_E − t_b)/(2t_E)]·L_bed` has a hard floor of 0.5·L_bed. Measured values span L_MTZ/L = 0.519 to 0.589 — CV 4.0 % against 85 % for t_b. Its newly significant p-values reflect that a 4 % spread became resolvable with 16 points, not that the MTZ moves much. **Do not present L_MTZ effects as physically large.**
3. **q_dyn is not currently measurable** at the precision needed (§3.3): 78 % pure error, replicates differing 3×. The n = 9 conclusion that concentration drives capacity is **withdrawn** pending a fixed-endpoint acquisition protocol. This is the highest-value experimental fix available.
4. **One run has no t_E** (`2026-07-29-conc5-flow0.05`, never reaches C/C₀ = 0.95). Handled pairwise, never imputed; four responses run at n = 15.
5. **One file is mis-named** (`2026-07-22-conc10-flow0.10` is a 0.15 lpm run). Corrected from metadata and confirmed physically, but the file should be renamed at source.
6. **The uniform MC prior is an assumption**, flagged as such. k₀, τ₀, τ₁, τ₂ are log-normal-shaped; a log-uniform prior would sample them more defensibly and reduce the off-ridge rejection rate.
7. **Tier-2 results are conditional on one operating point** (Q = 0.10 lpm, C₀ = 100 580 ppm). Parameter rankings could reorder at the corners.
8. **Do not pool the two datasets for inference** (§3.1). Bed length and column i.d. differ systematically.
9. **`ρ_p` remains unknown**, so ε and interstitial velocity are not physical. Every velocity here is *superficial*. Owner: lab / Stampi-Bombelli.
10. **What would most improve this analysis, in order:** (i) fix the q_dyn acquisition endpoint; (ii) supply geometry for `2026-07-17-conc15-flow0.15` and re-record it without the dropout; (iii) add a second replicate to the (0.05, 15 %) and (0.15, 15 %) cells to fully balance the design; (iv) re-fit M10/M11 on `(κ, τ₀, h)`.
