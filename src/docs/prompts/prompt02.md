## PROMPT (send as-is — identical for both dispatches; do not paste the existing proof into context)

Don't search the internet. This is a test to see how well you can craft non-trivial, novel and creative proofs given a "Ψ-Quadrature Consistency and Error-Bound" math problem. Provide a full unconditional proof or disproof of the problem.

REMEMBER — this unconditional argument may require non-trivial, creative and novel elements.

### The problem

A packed-bed CO₂ adsorption column is governed by the coupled system (mass balance, LDF kinetics, nonlinear isotherm, energy balance) given in full below. Two independent routes predict its breakthrough behaviour in the constant-pattern regime.

**Route A — derived shape.** Assume $D_L=0$, isothermal, favorable isotherm $Q(c)$. The wave speed $v_{RH}=uc_f/[\varepsilon c_f+\alpha_bQ(c_f)]$ follows from a Rankine–Hugoniot jump condition (not assumed), and the wave shape $\varphi(\eta)$, $\eta=z-v_{RH}t$, follows from solving the ODE $\varphi'(\eta)=-\tfrac{kc_f}{v_{RH}q_f}[Q(\varphi)-\tfrac{q_f}{c_f}\varphi]$, which for Langmuir $Q$ integrates to $\ln w-(1+bc_f)\ln(1-w)=-\tfrac{kbc_f}{v_{RH}}(\eta-\eta_0)$, $w=\varphi/c_f$ — asymmetric, with distinct leading/tail e-folding rates whenever $bc_f\ne0$.

**Route B — assumed shape.** Define the driving force $\psi=q^*-q$. Under the chain-rule substitution $\partial y/\partial t=(\partial y/\partial q)(\partial q/\partial t)$ and two coefficients $\gamma_q,\beta$ that are $\approx1$ specifically for Langmuir isotherms (proved by direct evaluation, not assumed, for the reference system this method was validated on), $\psi$ satisfies the *linear* ODE $\partial\psi/\partial t=-\beta k\psi\Rightarrow\psi\propto e^{-\beta kt}$. A **symmetric two-branch exponential ansatz** is then stitched around the stoichiometric time $t_s$ in a shifted "corrected time" coordinate $\tau=(t-z/u_f)-t_s$ — symmetric by construction, regardless of $bc_f$ — and every field ($F,c,Q,T^G,q,T^S$) is reconstructed by marching Riemann-sum quadratures built on the exponential Green's-function kernel of the linear transport operator, seeded by that ansatz. No ODE for the *shape* is solved; the shape is posited and the bookkeeping is exact.

**Claim.** Set route B's free parameters equal to route A's derived quantities ($u_f\to v_{RH}$, $t_s\to t_{st}=L/v_{RH}$, isothermal, single-component Langmuir, $\gamma_q=\beta=1$). Then:

(a) *(Shared limit, easy direction — establish this first as a sanity check, not the main result.)* As $bc_f\to0$ (weak nonlinearity / Henry regime), both routes' predicted outlet curve converge to the same symmetric logistic $1/(1+e^{-kbc_f(t-t_{st})})$ — route A because its asymmetry vanishes ($\ell_+/\ell_-\to1$) as $bc_f\to0$, route B because its ansatz was symmetric all along and this is the regime where symmetry stops being a wrong assumption. Prove this shared limit rigorously (it should be a short, clean argument) and use it only to calibrate that both constructions are internally consistent before addressing (b)–(c), which are the substantial parts.

(b) *(Mesh-refinement convergence, away from the shared limit.)* Fix $bc_f=O(1)$ or larger (genuinely favorable/nonlinear). As route B's marching mesh is refined ($\Delta t,\Delta z\to0$), does its discrete profile converge to *route A's* exact closed-form profile $\varphi(\eta)$, to *its own* (wrong, symmetric) continuum ansatz limit, or to neither? If it converges to its own ansatz limit rather than to route A, quantify the resulting error floor — the gap between route B's converged-but-wrong-shape prediction and route A's true asymmetric profile — as an explicit, computable function of $bc_f$, and show whether that floor vanishes as $bc_f\to0$ (consistent with part (a)) and how it grows as $bc_f$ increases.

(c) *(Non-isothermal extension — where route A itself has no closed form, MM Part D.6.)* Restore Arrhenius coupling $b(T)$. Route A no longer has a closed-form comparator at all: the concentration and thermal waves generally travel at different speeds ($v_{RH}$ vs. $v_{th}=\gamma_hu$), producing two-front structures (documented numerically: an adiabatic run whose outlet stalls on a plateau at $c/c_f\approx0.955$ while $T$ remains $18.9\,$K above ambient, MM Appendix V.5) that a *single*-front ansatz cannot represent by construction, symmetric or not. Does route B's marching machinery (its heat-flux and temperature quadratures) still converge to *something* as the mesh refines, and if so, is there any sense in which that limit can be shown to bound, or fail to bound, the true two-front error — given that the only available comparator in this regime is not a closed form but the independently-validated finite-volume solution already in the repository (`mechanistic_verify.py`, reproduced below)? In particular, address whether route B's ansatz — built for a *single* front — can ever converge to a *two*-front solution under mesh refinement alone, or whether this is a structural limitation no amount of resolution fixes (i.e. whether the relevant error here is a discretisation error at all, or a model-class error that mesh refinement is powerless against).

Prove the claim in full generality for the system given, or disprove each part by exhibiting the specific mechanism that breaks it — for (b), identify which step in route B's "assume-then-march" construction is where the wrong-shape information gets baked in irrecoverably (is it Eq. 1's ansatz itself, or something introduced later in the marching quadratures of Eqs. 2–8 that could in principle correct an initially-wrong shape but doesn't); for (c), be explicit about whether "convergence" is even the right question to ask of a single-front scheme applied to a genuinely two-front problem, or whether the honest answer is that route B's applicability is bounded by a regime check (e.g. on $\Phi\cdot\delta_1$ or the front-speed ratio $v_{th}/v_{RH}$) rather than by a convergence rate.

Your answer must directly engage with:
- whether there is a general consistency/Lax-equivalence-type principle governing when a marching scheme built on an *assumed* (rather than *derived*) self-similar shape can still converge to the *true* solution, and if so what property of the ansatz and the marching operator that principle depends on;
- the fact that route B's own derivation (Appendix A/B of the source paper) proves $\gamma_q\approx1,\beta\approx1$ *specifically and only* for a Langmuir-type system at the operating point it was evaluated at — meaning the "$\approx1$" approximation route B relies on to even get a linear driving-force ODE is itself isotherm- and possibly operating-point-dependent, and any error-floor result for part (b) needs to state whether it is charging that error to the shape-ansatz mismatch, to the $\gamma_q,\beta\approx1$ approximation, or to genuine discretisation error, since conflating the three would make the "floor is a function of $bc_f$ alone" claim in part (b) untrustworthy;
- do not simply cite that convergence of marching/quadrature schemes to ODE solutions "is known" in general — construct or refute the argument from the specific governing equations and specific ansatz given below; generic finite-difference consistency theorems do not automatically transfer to a scheme built on an assumed rather than derived self-similar shape, and that gap is exactly what must be closed or exposed.

### Information you may or may not need

**Governing system (single-component CO₂, packed bed).**
$$\varepsilon c_t+uc_z=\varepsilon D_Lc_{zz}-\alpha_bq_t,\qquad q_t=k(q^*(c,T)-q),\qquad C_hT_t+u\rho_gc_{p,g}T_z=\lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}T_{zz}+\alpha_b(-\Delta H)q_t-\tfrac{4h_w}{d_{\mathrm{col}}}(T-T_{\mathrm{wall}}),$$
Toth closure $q^*(c,T)=n_s(T)b(T)c/[1+(b(T)c)^{t_T}]^{1/t_T}$, $b(T)=b_0\exp[\tfrac{\Delta H_0}{RT_0}(\tfrac{T_0}{T}-1)]$; Langmuir sub-case $Q(c)=q_mbc/(1+bc)$. Clean-bed IC, Danckwerts inlet, zero-gradient outlet.

**Route A closed forms.** $v_{RH}=uc_f/[\varepsilon c_f+\alpha_bQ(c_f)]$; $t_{st}=L/v_{RH}=\int_0^\infty(1-c(L,t)/c_f)dt$ (Corollary B.1, model-free, holds for *any* $D_L,k$ — usable as an independent check on route B's $t_s$ even outside the constant-pattern regime); Langmuir wave as above, tail rates $\ell_+^{-1}\propto Q'(0)-q_f/c_f$, $\ell_-^{-1}\propto q_f/c_f-Q'(c_f)$, equal only as $bc_f\to0$.

**Route B mechanics.** $\psi=q^*-q$; $\partial\psi/\partial t=-\beta k\psi$ (linear, from Appendix-B chain-rule substitution, valid where $\gamma_q,\beta\approx1$); two-branch exponential ansatz in corrected time $\tau=(t-z/u_f)-t_s$; Riemann-sum marching quadratures for flux/heat-flux/mole-fraction/gas-temperature; loading and solid temperature reconstructed algebraically; front kinematics $t_s=\tfrac{\varepsilon L}{u}(1+\Lambda)$, $\Lambda=\alpha_bq_f/(\varepsilon c_f)$ — identical, term for term, to route A's $t_{st}$ once $\Lambda$ is recognised as MM's own capacity ratio $\alpha$.

**Relaxation-expansion background (possibly relevant to part (b)'s order-of-convergence argument).** For large-but-finite $Da=kL/u$, a Chapman–Enskog expansion of the LDF ODE gives $q=Q(c)-k^{-1}\partial_tQ(c)+O(k^{-2})$, i.e. finite kinetics behaves as extra effective dispersion $D_{\mathrm{kin}}\propto v_{\mathrm{eff}}^2/k$ (a van Deemter C-term, derived not assumed) on top of $D_L$. This is an existing instance of "assume-equilibrium-then-correct-perturbatively" acquiring a systematic, computable error term in a small parameter — check whether route B's own small-parameter structure ($1-\gamma_q$, $1-\beta$, both $\to0$ specifically in the Langmuir limit) admits an analogous expansion, and whether the mesh-refinement limit and this small-parameter limit commute.

**Numerical ground truth for part (c) (no closed form exists here, per the governing model's own Part D.6).** An independently validated finite-volume Method-of-Lines solver (upwind convection, central dispersion, Danckwerts inlet as exact face flux) reproduces: the linear-isotherm analytical solution to $<1\%$ relative $L^2$ error; the Rankine–Hugoniot front speed to $0.020\%$ and the first-moment stoichiometric time to $<0.005\%$; the exact Langmuir travelling wave to $0.30\%$ RMS with zero fitted parameters; discrete mass-balance drift of $4.6\times10^{-5}$; and, for the full non-isothermal Toth case, a documented two-front structure (adiabatic run: outlet concentration plateaus at $c/c_f\approx0.955$ while outlet temperature remains $+18.9\,$K above ambient) that persists at fine mesh resolution — i.e. this is not itself a discretisation artefact to be refined away, it is the true solution's structure.

**Empirical corroboration (context, not proof-admissible evidence).** This project's own curve-fitting library, applied to five real packed-bed CO₂ breakthrough runs at 4.7–15.1% CO₂ on a PEI-functionalised sorbent, finds all five measured curves strongly right-skewed/asymmetric — fractal/asymmetric model families beat the symmetric logistic by roughly $6\times$ in RMSE. This is consistent with route A's prediction that asymmetry should be the norm once $bc_f$ is not small, and inconsistent with route B's ansatz being generally adequate — but the real runs are also non-isothermal, non-dilute, and finite-$Pe$, so this observation cannot be used as evidence inside the proof itself; it only motivates why the question is worth answering rigorously rather than dismissing route B outright.
