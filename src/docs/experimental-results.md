# Experimental Results and Analysis

*CO₂ Adsorption Breakthrough in a Packed-Bed Column Charged with PEI–SiO₂ Granules*

The layout of this section follows Hu et al. (2024), *A critical review of breakthrough models with analytical solutions in a fixed-bed column* (J. Water Process Eng. 59, 105065), which organises a breakthrough study as: column/MTZ description → candidate model catalogue → goodness-of-fit and model-discrimination framework (AICc, nested *F*-test, Adj. R²) → parametric trends → interpretation. The same spine is used here, with the review's aqueous-phase worked examples replaced by the five bench runs performed on this project's column.

Every quantity reported below is traceable to a stored artefact: a `breakthrough_out/<run>/results_<run>.csv` cell, a generated figure, or a raw input file in `src/solver/data/new runs/`. Quantities are labelled **measured** (read from the raw breakthrough log), **fitted** (a regression parameter), or **derived** (computed from a measured curve by the definitions in `breakthrough_fit/performance.py`). No values were hand-tuned. Trends are stated only over the operating range actually swept. The figures referenced are regenerated by `python -m breakthrough_fit.assemble_may_prompt`; per-run diagnostic plots (P1–P7) by `python new_runs_pipeline.py`.

### 1.2 Operating-time definitions

Following Hu et al. (2024) Eqs. 9–12 and the `breakthrough_fit/performance.py` implementation:

- **t_BT** — breakthrough time, C_t/C₀ = 0.05, **derived** by linear interpolation.
- **t₅₀** — mid-point time, C_t/C₀ = 0.50, **derived**.
- **t_E** — exhaustion (saturation) time, C_t/C₀ = 0.95, **derived**.
- **q_dyn** — dynamic capacity, (*Q C₀* / *m*) ∫₀^{t_E} (1 − C/C₀) d*t* [mol kg⁻¹], **derived**.
- **L_MTZ** — mass-transfer zone length, [1 − (t_E − t_BT)/(2 t_E)] · *L_bed*, **derived**.
- **ψ** — stoichiometric efficiency, *t_BT* / *t\** where *t\** = ∫₀^∞ (1 − C/C₀) d*t*, **derived**.

**All five runs reached C/C₀ ≥ 0.95** before data collection ended; *t_E*, *L_MTZ*, and the fully-truncated *q_dyn* are therefore **measured** directly rather than model-extrapolated. This distinguishes these runs from partial breakthrough curves (Hu et al. 2024 §5.2) where the exhaustion threshold is never crossed.

## 3. Breakthrough-Curve Phenomenology

### 3.1 The mass-transfer zone

Following Hu et al. (2024) §2, the MTZ is the axial zone where C/C₀ rises from ~0 to ~1. At breakthrough, the MTZ reaches the outlet; the recorded C/C₀(t) trace is the MTZ passing the column exit. A narrow MTZ produces a steep S-shaped curve (high stoichiometric efficiency); a broad MTZ produces a drawn-out sigmoidal curve with early breakthrough and poor capacity utilisation.

For the five bench runs:

**Table 0b — Derived performance metrics (all from `performance.py`, Formula sources as labelled).**

| Run | *t_BT* (s) | *t₅₀* (min) | *t_E* (min) | *q_dyn* (mol kg⁻¹) | *L_MTZ* (cm) | ψ (–) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| run 3 | 18.3 | 5.48 | 58.4 | 0.555 | 10.55 | 0.018 |
| run 4 | 34.1 | 12.60 | 77.7 | 0.810 | 10.73 | 0.017 |
| run 5 | 13.6 | 3.83 | 31.7 | 0.552 | 10.68 | 0.019 |
| run 6 | 19.0 | 4.00 | 39.3 | 0.885 | 10.84 | 0.025 |
| run 8 | 26.7 | 4.10 | 34.5 | 0.787 | 10.89 | 0.039 |

### 3.2 Structural observations

**Very short breakthrough times.** t_BT ranges from 13.6 to 34.1 seconds across runs. For context, the empty-tube gas transit time at *U* = 4.41 cm s⁻¹ through a 21 cm bed is ≈ 4.8 s; at ε ≈ 0.30, the interstitial transit time is ≈ 1.4 s. A t_BT of ~18–34 s represents 5–12 interstitial transit times — consistent with a system where the equilibrium front is retarded but the column length is short relative to the MTZ width.

**Broad MTZ (≈ 50 % of bed length).** L_MTZ ≈ 10.5–10.9 cm in all five runs, occupying approximately half the 21 cm packed bed. This indicates that the CO₂ adsorption front is never fully sharp: the column operates in a regime where the MTZ spans most of the available bed, with limited separation into a clean saturated zone and a clean pre-breakthrough zone.

**Low stoichiometric efficiency.** ψ = 0.017–0.039 across all runs. Under ideal plug-flow with instantaneous equilibrium, ψ → 1; the measured values reflect both the broad MTZ and the very early 5 % threshold relative to the total equilibration time (t_BT/t_E ≈ 0.3–0.7 %).

**Dynamic capacity in the physically plausible range.** q_dyn = 0.55–0.89 mol kg⁻¹ (corrected 2026-05-31; a prior ×1000 unit error in `performance.py` put these values ~1000× too low). For comparison, PEI–SiO₂ sorbents typically show working capacities of 0.5–2.5 mol kg⁻¹ at 5–15 % CO₂ and ambient temperature — the measured values are consistent. An independent back-calculation via the Yoon–Nelson *q₀* parameter gives q₀ ≈ 0.28–0.46 mol kg⁻¹ per unit carbon loading, confirming order-of-magnitude agreement.

**All runs fully saturated.** The maximum measured C/C₀ reaches or exceeds 0.95 in all five runs, so *t_E* and *q_dyn* are direct integrations over actual data rather than model extrapolations.

## 5. Nested F-test: BA/Logistic (M01) vs Fractal-BA (M23)

The only valid nested pair in the prompt model set is M01 ⊂ M23 (M23 collapses to M01 at *h* = 0). The *F*-test on the RSS reduction tests whether the fractal exponent *h* is warranted by the data. Full Table 4: `src/img/generated/may_prompt/table4_ftest.csv`.

**Table 4 — Nested F-test results.**

| Run | RSS(M01) | RSS(M23) | *n* | *F* statistic | *p*-value | Fractal warranted? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| run 3 | 1.619 | 0.0532 | 383 | 11 184 | 6.1 × 10⁻²⁸⁴ | **Yes** |
| run 4 | 5.503 | 0.182 | 1432 | 41 680 | ≈ 0 | **Yes** |
| run 5 | 3.293 | 0.148 | 821 | 17 416 | ≈ 0 | **Yes** |
| run 6 | 0.921 | 0.0165 | 285 | 15 490 | 1.8 × 10⁻²⁴⁸ | **Yes** |
| run 8 | 0.688 | 0.0122 | 255 | 13 980 | 9.3 × 10⁻²²³ | **Yes** |

The fractal term is decisive in every run (*F* > 10 000, *p* ≪ 0.001 throughout). The RSS reduction from M01 to M23 is 94–97 % in all five runs, meaning the symmetric logistic leaves most of the explainable variance uncaptured. The fractal exponent *h* (from the M23/M11 fits) is in the range 0.42–0.87 — far above zero and below unity, consistent with diffusion-limited uptake on a heterogeneous amine surface (Hu et al. 2024 §5.4; the fractal exponent encodes the power-law decay of the apparent rate constant over time).

**Caveat on homoscedasticity.** Because C/C₀ ∈ [0,1], measurement variance is structurally bounded (≈ μ(1−μ)), violating the homoscedasticity assumption underlying OLS-based *F*-tests and standard AIC. The *F* statistics here are enormous (> 10 000), so the conclusion is robust even accounting for this violation; but for marginal cases, a beta-regression or logit-transformed approach would be more rigorous (Hu et al. 2024, §4 note).

## 6. Parametric Trends

### 6.1 Flow sweep at ~10 % CO₂ (runs 4/5/6)

The cleanest sub-experiment is the 10 % CO₂ flow sweep: runs 4 (50 mL min⁻¹), 5 (100 mL min⁻¹), 6 (150 mL min⁻¹). Concentrations differ slightly (97 800, 95 420, 102 140 ppm); this minor variation (within ~7 %) is noted where it could affect interpretation.

| Metric | run 4 (50) | run 5 (100) | run 6 (150) | Trend |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *U* (cm s⁻¹) | 1.47 | 2.94 | 4.41 | monotone 3× |
| *t₅₀* (min) | 12.60 | 3.83 | 4.00 | ↓ with flow |
| *t_E* (min) | 77.7 | 31.7 | 39.3 | non-monotone |
| *q_dyn* (mol kg⁻¹) | 0.810 | 0.552 | 0.885 | non-monotone |
| *L_MTZ* (cm) | 10.73 | 10.68 | 10.84 | ≈ constant |
| ψ (–) | 0.017 | 0.019 | 0.025 | ≈ flat |

**t₅₀ decreases with flow from 50 → 100 mL min⁻¹** (12.60 → 3.83 min, 3.3× reduction for a 2× flow increase) as expected from the reduced residence time. **Run 6 (150 mL min⁻¹) does not continue this trend**: t₅₀ = 4.00 min and t_E = 39.3 min — both slightly longer than run 5. The anomaly may reflect run-to-run variability in initial conditions (e.g. slight pre-loading or timing offset) rather than a physical reversal; t_BT (19.0 s for run 6 vs 13.6 s for run 5) carries the same inconsistency, and at these very short timescales (seconds) the uncertainty in the crossing-time interpolation is large relative to the value.

**L_MTZ and ψ** show negligible variation across the flow sweep (~0.15 cm spread in L_MTZ, 0.008 spread in ψ). This suggests that over this flow range the MTZ width tracks the bed length approximately, with the column saturating to ~50 % of its length in all three runs.

**q_dyn** does not decrease monotonically with flow. Run 6 (150 mL min⁻¹) has the highest q_dyn (0.885 mol kg⁻¹), contrary to the expected trend of lower capacity utilisation at higher velocity. The slight C₀ difference between runs (run 6 has the highest C₀ at 102 140 ppm) and the slightly longer t_E in run 6 vs run 5 both inflate the integral for run 6. Until the ~10 % CO₂ sweep is repeated with tighter C₀ control, the flow–q_dyn relationship cannot be reliably stated.

### 6.2 Concentration effect (runs 3, 5, 8 at ~100 mL min⁻¹)

Run 5 (95 420 ppm, 100 mL min⁻¹) and run 8 (150 630 ppm, 100 mL min⁻¹) permit a concentration comparison at fixed flow; run 3 (47 400 ppm, 150 mL min⁻¹) differs also in flow so is not a clean concentration comparison.

| Metric | run 5 (95 420 ppm) | run 8 (150 630 ppm) |
|---|---|---|
| *t₅₀* (min) | 3.83 | 4.10 |
| *t_E* (min) | 31.7 | 34.5 |

Higher CO₂ concentration increases *q_dyn* (0.552 → 0.787 mol kg⁻¹), consistent with a steeper isotherm driving force loading more CO₂ per unit sorbent mass before saturation. Stoichiometric efficiency ψ also increases with concentration (0.019 → 0.039), suggesting that the higher driving force sharpens the wave front slightly, allowing a larger fraction of equilibrium capacity to be used before the 5 % breakthrough threshold is crossed.

### 6.3 Fractal exponent *h* trend

The M23 fractal exponent *h* carries information about the heterogeneity of adsorption kinetics:

| Run | *h* (M23) | *h* (M11) | *C₀* (ppm) | *U* (cm/s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| run 4 | 0.755 | 0.418 | 97 800 | 1.47 |
| run 5 | 0.799 | 0.472 | 95 420 | 2.94 |

| run 8 | 0.872 | 0.563 | 150 630 | 2.94 |

This is consistent with Hu et al. Run 8 (higher concentration at same flow as run 5) also shows a higher *h*, suggesting that higher driving force similarly accelerates the transition to diffusion-limited uptake on heterogeneous sites.

## 7. Interpretation

### 7.1 Symmetry diagnosis

All five breakthrough curves are **right-asymmetric**: the rising tail (C/C₀ rising from 0.50 to 0.95) is slower relative to the initial rise (0 to 0.50). Evidence:

- Clark *n* = 1.01 in all runs (much less than 2, the symmetric Yoon–Nelson limit).
- MDR *a* = 1.18–1.41, all > 1 (symmetric MDR: *a* = 1).
- Fractal exponent *h* = 0.42–0.87, statistically decisive in every run (§5).
- The single symmetric logistic (M01) fits worst among complete-curve models in all five runs; its residuals show a systematic S-shaped bias at the early rising and late tail regions.

The asymmetry is consistent with mass-transfer resistance on heterogeneous PEI–SiO₂ granules: fast chemisorption on exposed amine sites is followed by slower diffusion into the pellet interior, making the effective rate constant decrease over time as the more accessible sites are consumed.

### 7.2 Model hierarchy

From best to worst among the nine prompt models:

**M23 (Fractal-BA / Fractal Yoon–Nelson) ≈ M14 (Weibull) >> M04 (MDR) > M02 (Clark) ≈ M06 (Gudermannian) ≈ M01 (Logistic) > M07 (Error function) >> M16 (Klinkenberg)**

M23 and M14 are statistically indistinguishable (both Adj. R² > 0.996 in all five runs). The two-parameter Weibull model achieves near-parity with the three-parameter fractal extension, implying that the stretched-exponential Weibull form captures the asymmetry almost as efficiently as the explicit fractal rate. However, M23 has a direct physical interpretation (fractal kinetics: *k* ∝ *t*^(−*h*)) while the Weibull shape parameter *k* is purely phenomenological.

From the **wider 24-model library**, M11 (Fractal Error-Function) ranked first or second in all five runs with a marginal AICc advantage over M23. Both are Hu (2024) fractal models; the choice between them does not change any physical conclusion. M11 is recommended for the final mechanistic comparison (Gate C) because its error-function kernel aligns with the dispersion physics of the packed-bed PDE.

The Clark *n* → 1 result (§4.2) means the Clark model is not capturing the asymmetry in the expected way. Clark at *n* = 2 is Yoon–Nelson; at *n* = 1 the exponent 1/(n−1) → ∞ produces an artificially steep S-curve. The optimiser is finding a region where *n* = 1 minimises RSS, suggesting the data are not well-described by a Freundlich-exponent mechanism.

### 7.3 Parameter trends with operating conditions

For the flow sweep (runs 4/5/6 at ~10 % CO₂):

- The Yoon–Nelson characteristic time τ from M01 decreases with flow: 1004 s (50 mL min⁻¹) → 343 s (100) → 350 s (150). The expected Thomas/YN scaling *τ* ∝ *q₀ m* / (*ν C₀*) would predict *τ* ∝ 1/*ν* (inverse flow), matching the 50→100 step but not the 100→150 step, consistent with the broader anomaly noted in §6.1.

- The fractal M11 characteristic time τ₀ also decreases with flow: 743 s (50) → 226 s (100) → 223 s (150). Runs 5 and 6 are nearly identical in τ₀ despite a 2× flow difference.

- The fractal exponent *h* increases with flow (0.418 → 0.472 → 0.549), interpreted in §6.3 as increased kinetic heterogeneity at shorter contact times.

### 7.4 Wolborska and Klinkenberg validity

**Wolborska (M05)** is an exponential function valid only in the initial rising portion (Hu et al. 2024, §5.6 explicit warning): it is mathematically equivalent to the oversimplified Bohart–Adams form and is invalid for fitting complete breakthrough curves. The early-window Adj. R² of 0.925–0.956 (runs 3/4/5) does not extend to the full curve. This model is retained in the tables per the prompt requirement but must not be used for capacity or efficiency estimates.

**Klinkenberg (M16)** requires ζ ≥ 2 and τ_K ≥ 1 for its asymptotic approximation to be valid (Klinkenberg 1948; Hu et al. 2024 §3). These conditions have not been verified for this column — the lumped mass-transfer parameter *K_f·a* converges to its lower bound in all five fits, indicating the model is not calibrated by this data. Klinkenberg is excluded from model-preference conclusions.

### 7.5 Limitations of empirical models

Models M01, M02, M04, M06, M07, M14, M23 are all phenomenological: their lumped parameters (k_YN, τ, h, a, etc.) embed unmeasured physical processes and **cannot be extrapolated outside the fitted operating range** without a mechanistic model. Specifically:

- k_YN from M01 combines axial dispersion, mass-transfer resistance, and isotherm linearisation into a single rate constant; it cannot constrain the LDF model for Gate C.
- h from M23/M11 is the most informative parameter for mechanistic follow-up: it quantifies the degree of intraparticle diffusion limitation, and can serve as an empirical calibration target for the LDF mass-transfer coefficient k_LDF in the full 4-PDE model (Stampi-Bombelli 2024).
- q_dyn from the integral is physically meaningful and should be compared to the Toth isotherm equilibrium loading at the inlet concentration once ρ_p and ε are confirmed.

---

## 8. Summary of Supported Conclusions

1. **All five runs are fully saturated.** t_E is directly measured (not model-extrapolated) in every run.
2. **Breakthrough curves are right-asymmetric in all runs.** Clark *n* → 1, MDR *a* = 1.2–1.4, fractal *h* = 0.42–0.87 (decisive F-test in all five runs).
3. **Fractal models (M23, M11, M10) dominate.** Among the nine prompt models, M23 (Fractal-BA) ranks first and M14 (Weibull) near-equal. From the 24-model library, M11 (Fractal Error-Function) is the most consistent top performer.
4. **Dynamic capacity is physically plausible:** q_dyn = 0.55–0.89 mol kg⁻¹ (PEI–SiO₂ literature range: 0.5–2.5 mol kg⁻¹ at 5–15 % CO₂, ambient T).
5. **Flow sweep (runs 4/5/6 at ~10 % CO₂)** shows the expected decrease in t₅₀ from 50 → 100 mL min⁻¹ but an anomaly at 150 mL min⁻¹ (runs 5 and 6 have near-identical t₅₀ and τ₀); a controlled repeat is required before the 150 mL min⁻¹ operating point can be interpreted.
6. **Column operates with broad MTZ (≈ 50 % of bed, ψ ≈ 0.02–0.04).** Very poor capacity utilisation at breakthrough for all five runs, attributable to the short packed-bed length (21 cm) and high superficial velocities relative to the MTZ width.

## 9. What the Data Do Not Yet Support

- **Unambiguous flow trend at 150 mL min⁻¹** (run 5 vs run 6 anomaly in §6.1) — requires a controlled repeat.
- **Physical ε and v values** — ρ_p is an open input; ε is floored at 0.30 and v is unreliable until ρ_p is confirmed.
- **Ergun consistency check on ε** — the measured outlet-vs-inlet flow deficit (runs 3/4/5) could supply an independent ε estimate but is not yet implemented.
- **Extrapolation beyond the swept range** — empirical model parameters are not mechanistically constrained; k_LDF and the Toth isotherm must come from Stampi-Bombelli 2024 (Gate C).
