Evans §3.1–3.2 · ut + c(x) ux = 0

Method of characteristics · Three textbook problems · The caustic warning for non-constant carrier velocity

1 · Framework: method of characteristics

We solve the Cauchy problem

\[ u_t + c(x)\,u_x = 0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\;t>0, \qquad u(x,0)=g(x). \tag{P}\]

The left side is the directional derivative of \(u\) along the vector \((1,\,c(x))\in\mathbb{R}^2_{t,x}\). We seek curves \((t,\,x(t))\) along which \(u\) is constant; the tangent must be proportional to \((1,c(x))\). The full characteristic system is

\[\dot t = 1,\qquad \dot x = c(x), \qquad \dot u = 0. \tag{char}\]

Since \(\dot u=0\), the solution is

\[\boxed{u(x,t) = g\!\left(x_0(x,t)\right),}\]

where \(x_0(x,t)\) is the foot of the characteristic: the unique \(x_0\) such that the solution of the base ODE \(dx/dt=c(x),\; x(0)=x_0\) satisfies \(x(t;\,x_0)=x\).

Jacobian and the compression criterion

Set \(J(t;x_0)=\partial x(t;x_0)/\partial x_0\). Differentiating the ODE in \(x_0\):

\[\frac{dJ}{dt}=c'\!\left(x(t;x_0)\right)J,\quad J(0)=1 \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; J(t;x_0)=\exp\!\Bigl(\int_0^t c'\!\left(x(s;x_0)\right)ds\Bigr).\]

The spatial gradient satisfies \(u_x = g'(x_0)/J\). A caustic (characteristic fold) occurs where \(J\to 0\):

  • \(c'(\cdot)>0\) along the characteristic \(\Rightarrow\) \(J\nearrow\infty\): characteristics spread, gradients dilute.
  • \(c'(\cdot)<0\) along the characteristic \(\Rightarrow\) \(J\searrow 0\): characteristics compress, gradients concentrate.

Case 1 · \(c=1\) uniform translation

Characteristic ODE. \(\;dx/dt=1\) with \(x(0)=x_0\) gives \(x(t;x_0)=x_0+t\). Inverting: \(x_0=x-t\).

Solution.

\[\boxed{u(x,t)=g(x-t).}\]

The initial profile \(g\) translates rigidly to the right at speed 1. The Jacobian \(J=\exp(\int_0^t 0\,ds)=1\) is identically 1; characteristics are parallel straight lines and the map \(x_0\mapsto x_0+t\) is an isometry for every \(t\). The gradient \(u_x(x,t)=g'(x-t)\) is preserved in amplitude for all time — no compression, no expansion.

Fig. 1 · Parallel characteristics in the (t, x)-plane for \(c=1\); initial positions \(x_0\in\{-1,0,1,2,3\}\). Jacobian J ≡ 1 throughout.

Case 2 · \(c=x\) expanding transport

Characteristic ODE. \(\;dx/dt=x\) with \(x(0)=x_0\) gives \(x(t;x_0)=x_0 e^t\). Inverting: \(x_0=xe^{-t}\).

Solution.

\[\boxed{u(x,t)=g\!\left(xe^{-t}\right).}\]

Since \(c'(x)=1>0\) everywhere, the Jacobian \(J=e^t\to\infty\). Characteristics from distinct \(x_0\) spread exponentially and never cross. The origin \(x_0=0\) is the unique fixed characteristic. The gradient \(u_x=g'(xe^{-t})\cdot e^{-t}\) decays: expanding transport dilutes the profile amplitude over time.

Fig. 2 · Exponentially diverging characteristics for \(c=x\); five values \(x_0\in\{0.25,0.5,1,1.5,2\}\). J = eᵗ → ∞.

Case 3 · \(c=\sin x\) compressive / caustic

The ODE \(dx/dt=\sin x\) is autonomous with fixed points at \(x=n\pi\). Stability is governed by \(c'(n\pi)=\cos(n\pi)=(-1)^n\):

  • Unstable (\(n\) even, \(x=0,2\pi,\ldots\)): \(c'=+1>0\), characteristics diverge.
  • Stable (\(n\) odd, \(x=\pi,3\pi,\ldots\)): \(c'=-1<0\), characteristics converge — here the caustic lives.

Separating variables (for \(x\in(0,\pi)\), so \(\sin x>0\)):

\[\int\frac{dx}{\sin x}=\int\csc x\,dx = \ln\!\left|\tan\tfrac{x}{2}\right| = t + C.\]

To verify the antiderivative: \(\tfrac{d}{dx}\ln|\tan(x/2)|= \tfrac{1}{\tan(x/2)}\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}\sec^2(x/2)=\tfrac{1}{2\sin(x/2)\cos(x/2)}=\tfrac{1}{\sin x}=\csc x\). ✓

Applying \(x(0)=x_0\):

\[\ln\!\left|\tan\tfrac{x}{2}\right|-\ln\!\left|\tan\tfrac{x_0}{2}\right|=t \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \tan\tfrac{x(t)}{2}=e^t\tan\tfrac{x_0}{2}.\]

Inverting (for \(x\in(0,\pi)\)):

\[x_0(x,t)=2\arctan\!\Bigl(e^{-t}\tan\tfrac{x}{2}\Bigr).\]

Solution.

\[\boxed{u(x,t)=g\!\!\left(2\arctan\!\Bigl(e^{-t}\tan\tfrac{x}{2}\Bigr)\right).}\]

As \(t\to\infty\): \(e^{-t}\tan(x/2)\to 0\), so \(x_0\to 0\) — all points in \((0,\pi)\) trace back toward the unstable fixed point \(x=0\) as we run time backwards. Equivalently, running forward, all of \((0,\pi)\) is funnelled toward \(x=\pi\).

Jacobian and gradient blow-up

Differentiate the characteristic relation \(\tan(x/2)=e^t\tan(x_0/2)\) with respect to \(x_0\):

\[\tfrac{1}{2}\sec^2\!\tfrac{x}{2}\cdot J = \tfrac{1}{2}e^t\sec^2\!\tfrac{x_0}{2} \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; J=\frac{e^t\sec^2(x_0/2)}{1+e^{2t}\tan^2(x_0/2)}.\]

For large \(t\) with \(x_0\in(0,\pi)\) fixed, the denominator is dominated by the \(e^{2t}\) term:

\[J\;\sim\;\frac{e^t\sec^2(x_0/2)}{e^{2t}\tan^2(x_0/2)}=\frac{e^{-t}}{\sin^2(x_0/2)}\;\longrightarrow\;0.\]

Hence — even for smooth \(g\) — the spatial gradient blows up exponentially:

\[u_x(x,t)=\frac{g'(x_0)}{J}\;\sim\;g'(x_0)\,\sin^2\!\tfrac{x_0}{2}\cdot e^t\;\longrightarrow\;\infty.\]

The solution \(u\) itself stays bounded (it equals \(g(x_0)\)), but the gradient concentrates without bound. This is the linear precursor of the caustic.

Fig. 3 · Characteristics converge to x = π (dashed) from below (purple) and above (coral). Both families are funnelled into the stable manifold. Jacobian J → 0.

4 · Jacobian comparison and the caustic warning

c = 1

\(J\equiv 1\) for all time. Isometric. Gradient amplitude preserved exactly. No compression anywhere. Caustic impossible.

c = x

\(J=e^t\to\infty\). Expanding. Gradients dilute over time. Characteristics diverge; no fold. Caustic impossible.

c = sin x ⚠

\(J\to 0\) near \(x=\pi\). Compressive. Gradients grow exponentially. Proto-caustic even in the linear regime.

Fig. 4 · Jacobian J(t) for the three cases. The c = sin x curve (x₀ = 1) rises briefly while c′ > 0 (x < π/2), then decays exponentially once the characteristic enters the compressive zone (π/2, π) where c′ = cos x < 0.

The caustic warning. For \(c(x)=\sin x\) the carrier speed satisfies \(c'(x)=\cos x<0\) throughout \((\pi/2,\,3\pi/2)\). A wide band of initial data is focused toward \(x=\pi\) — like light converging through a lens — compressing the characteristic pencil into a single spatial ray. For the purely linear PDE the solution \(u=g(x_0)\) stays bounded, but the gradient \(u_x=g'(x_0)/J\) grows exponentially even before any nonlinear term is present.

As soon as a nonlinear adsorption term is appended, e.g. \[ u_t + \sin(x)\,u_x + n(u)\,u_x = 0, \] the shock formation time is controlled by the total compression rate \(c'(x)+n'(u)\,u_x\). The linear carrier already contributes \(c'(x)=-1\) in the compressive zone, so the effective shock time is \[t^*=\frac{-1}{\min_{x_0}\bigl[c'(x_0)+n'(g(x_0))\,g'(x_0)\bigr]}\] which is strictly shorter than the estimate obtained by ignoring the \(c'\) term. Caustics (shocks) form inside the column — well before the exit boundary — even for a weak adsorption isotherm. This is a warning specific to non-constant carrier velocity: the linear geometry of the flow is itself compressive, and nonlinearity needs only a small push to trigger finite-time blow-up.
